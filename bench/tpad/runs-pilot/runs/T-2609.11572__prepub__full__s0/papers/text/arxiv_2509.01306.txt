ABSTRACT
Temporal Information Retrieval (TIR) is a critical yet unresolved
task for modern search systems, retrieving documents that not only
satisfy a query’s information need but also adhere to its temporal
constraints. This task is shaped by two challenges:Relevance, ensur-
ing alignment with the query’s explicit temporal requirements, and
Recency, selecting the freshest document among multiple versions.
Existing methods often address the two challenges in isolation,
relying on brittle heuristics that fail in scenarios where temporal
requirements and staleness resistance are intertwined. To address
this gap, we introduce Re2Bench, a benchmark specifically designed
to disentangle and evaluate Relevance, Recency, and their hybrid
combination. Building on this foundation, we propose Re3, a unified
and lightweight framework that dynamically balances semantic and
temporal information through a query-aware gating mechanism.
On Re2Bench, Re3 achieves state-of-the-art results, leading in R@1
across all three subsets. Ablation studies with backbone sensitivity
tests confirm robustness, showing strong generalization across di-
verse encoders and real-world settings. This work provides both a
generalizable solution and a principled evaluation suite, advancing
the development of temporally aware retrieval systems. Re3 and
Re2Bench are available online.1

1 INTRODUCTION
Driven by the rapid expansion of digital data, Information Retrieval
(IR) has emerged as a central pillar of modern information systems,
responsible for efficiently retrieving the most relevant document
from a large document collection to answer user queries. Early IR
approaches were dominated by sparse retrieval methods such as
TF–IDF and BM25 [32, 33], which rank documents based on exact
lexical overlap and term statistics but often fail to capture deeper se-
mantic relationships. The advent of Transformer-based pre-trained
language models [19, 38] and their retrieval-oriented embedding
variants, such as ANCE [46] and other recent models [42, 43, 45],
has led to substantial advances on standard benchmarks such as MS
MARCO [4] and Natural Questions [22, 23]. These techniques, when
applied to IR as dense retrieval models, have demonstrated stronger
performance than traditional sparse retrieval approaches [8].
Despite the success in modeling semantic relationships between
queries and documents, dense retrieval models often struggle with
queries that involve temporal constraints [44]. This limitation has
motivated the study of Temporal Information Retrieval (TIR), a line
of research rooted in early work on temporal understanding [2, 28]
and later formalized in benchmarks [16, 17]. TIR aims to improve
retrieval accuracy for time-sensitive queries by enhancing temporal
understanding capabilities of retrievers. Early research modeled

However, real-world TIR scenarios extend far beyond the scope
of previous work. As illustrated in Figure 1, we formalize three
representative scenarios, each illustrating a distinct aspect of TIR.
In Scenario 1-Relevance, the query asks for residence of some-
one in a specific year, and a document is deemed relevant only
if it satisfies this temporal requirement. This corresponds to the
challenge of queries with explicit temporal requirements, where the
retriever must align time information of documents with temporal
requirement of the query. We refer to this challenge as Relevance.
Another example is Scenario 2-Recency, where the query asks
how many students would return to Salmond College for a sec-
ond year, and the retrieved candidates contain multiple versions
of similar statements, while they published at different times. This
corresponds to the challenge of staleness resistance, which means
the retriever must identify and return the most appropriate ver-
sion, typically the freshest, while remaining sensitive to semantic
relevance [30, 35]. We refer to this challenge as Recency.
At the same time, in real-world applications, hybrid cases fre-
quently arise where both Relevance and Recency are critical. For
example, a weather-forecast query may target a specific valid date,
yet the corpus often contains multiple editions issued at different
times. As illustrated in Scenario 3-Hybrid, addressing such cases
requires the document’s clue time to match the query’s temporal
requirements while selecting the most up-to-date version [20, 41].
Although recent studies have attempted to incorporate temporal
information into retrieval [ 1, 12, 13, 34], they often address sin-
gle challenges in TIR through task-specific heuristics, resulting in
inconsistent performance across diverse scenarios.
To overcome these limitations, we introduce Relevance & Re-
cency Retrieval(Re3), a universal, plug-and-play, and lightweight
framework for TIR. Re3 comprises three modular components: Time
Extraction, Time Encoding and Time Enhancement. This design
enables retrievers to effectively handle Relevance, Recency, and
Hybrid scenarios without extensive architectural changes. To rig-
orously evaluate TIR models, we construct the Relevance & Re-
cency Benchmark(Re2Bench), comprising Re2-Rel from TSQA-
Human [ 6] for temporal requirement alignment, Re2-Rec from
HoH [30] for staleness resistance, andRe2-Hyb, a newly built NOAA-
based [26, 27] dataset integrating two challenges.
On Re2Bench, Re3 leads R@1 in all scenarios(0.920 on Re2-Rel,
0.649 on Re2-Rec, 0.742 on Re2-Hyb), while keeping temporal dis-
persion competitive in the hybrid setting. In general, a learnable
gate that balances semantic and temporal information yields more
reliable retrieval than optimizing either alone.
In summary, our contributions are as follows:
• We refine the formulation of TIR by identifying two core chal-
lenges, Relevance and Recency, and formalizing three representa-
tive scenarios. Building on this, we constructRe2Bench: Relevance
& Recency Benchmark, a three-subset evaluation suite covering
all the scenarios, and we introduce two new metrics: TimeVar@K
for relevance and MFG@K for recency.
• We propose Re3: Relevance & Recency Retrieval , a novel and mod-
ular framework that can be seamlessly integrated with exist-
ing dense retrievers. Re3 tackles the dual challenges of TIR by
combining time extraction, multi-frequency time encoding, and
query-aware semantic–temporal fusion.
• Extensive experiments show that Re3 achieves state-of-the-art
results on all subsets of Re 2Bench, while maintaining robust
performance across diverse encoders, indicating strong general-
ization and practical applicability in real-world TIR.
2 RELATED WORK
2.1 Benchmarking Temporal-Aware Retrieval
Temporal Information Retrieval (TIR) demands benchmarks that
capture both temporal requirement alignment (Relevance) and stale-
ness resistance (Recency). Early efforts such as NTCIR Temporalia
(NTCIR-11/12) [16, 17] and the TREC Temporal Summarization
Track [3, 7] evaluated temporal relevance ranking in news corpora
but could not disentangle these two challenges. Later datasets inher-
ited this separation: TSQA [6] targets explicit temporal constraints
(Relevance), while HoH [30] emphasizes recency via historical doc-
ument versions, making it difficult to assess trade-offs [15, 25, 36].
Beyond corpus design, a parallel line of work studies temporal
query intent, profiling or classifying when temporal evidence is re-
quired [14, 18]. This line is further operationalized in Temporalia’s
TQIC subtask [16], which complements TIR evaluations but does
not jointly assess alignment and freshness. We introduce Re2Bench,
comprising three scenarios—pure Relevance, pure Recency, and
hybrid—that enable both isolated and joint evaluation.
2.2 Modeling Temporality in Dense Retrieval
Classical IR systems rely on sparse retrievers like TF–IDF [33] and
BM25 [32], which match terms exactly and fail to capture tem-
porality. Earlier time-aware ranking integrated temporal priors

models [24], recency-sensitive ranking for web search [10], and es-
timation methods for ranking recent information [11]. The shift to
dense retrieval with pre-trained language models encodes queries
and documents into a shared embedding space, improving semantic
relevance [19, 21, 46] but ignoring temporal information essential
in TIR. Recent time-aware dense retrievers fall into two camps: (i)
feature-engineering, e.g., TempRetriever [1], which embeds times-
tamps directly but risks over-coupling semantic and temporal fea-
tures; and (ii) heuristic-fusion, e.g., TSM [13] and TempRALM [12],
which separate representations and fuse them via fixed rules, limit-
ing adaptivity to query needs and hurting hybrid performance. In
contrast to these methods, Re3 instead uses a learnable, query-aware
gating mechanism to adaptively balance semantic and temporal
information during re-ranking, yielding robust, generalizable gains
across diverse temporal retrieval scenarios.
3 PRELIMINARY
3.1 Problem Definition
We formally define Temporal Information Retrieval (TIR) as follows.
A query is a tuple𝑄 =(𝑐𝑞,𝑡𝑞), where𝑐𝑞 denotes the query content
and𝑡𝑞 specifies its temporal requirement (e.g., a time point or range),
hereafter referred to as thequery time. A document is represented as
a tuple𝐷 =(𝑐𝑑,𝑡𝑐,𝑡𝑑), where𝑐𝑑 denotes content of the document,
𝑡𝑐 captures the time information expressed in the content, hereafter
referred to as theclue time. And𝑡𝑑 denotes the publication or update
time of the document, reflecting its freshness.
Intuitively, a Temporal Information Retrieval (TIR) task aims to
identify, from a large document collection, the document that best
answers a given query while ensuring that the retrieved content
satisfies the query’s temporal constraints. Formally, given a query
𝑄 and a document collection𝐷★, the TIR task is to produce a ranked
list of documents by computing a retrieval score𝑆(𝑄,𝐷) for each
𝐷∈ 𝐷★ and sorting them in descending order based on 𝑆(𝑄,𝐷).
The score should jointly reflect the semantic match between 𝑐𝑞
and𝑐𝑑 as well as the constraints arising from the query’s temporal
requirement and the need for staleness resistance.
3.2 Core Challenges: Relevance & Recency
An effective TIR retriever must address two intertwined challenges:
Relevance. For queries with an explicit temporal requirement𝑡𝑞,
the retriever should select documents whose clue time𝑡𝑐 satisfies
this requirement. We define Align(𝑡𝑞,𝑡𝑐) as a temporal alignment
function that quantifies the proximity between𝑡𝑞 and𝑡𝑐. This func-
tion serves as a relevance-oriented feature for the retriever, guiding
it to better align a document’s clue time with the query’s tempo-
ral requirement. Neglecting this alignment can lead to returning
semantically relevant yet temporally mismatched results.
Recency. When multiple versions of a semantically similar doc-
ument are available over time, the retriever must identify the most
appropriate version, while guarding against indiscriminate recency
bias. We defineFresh(𝑡𝑑,𝑡 ref) as a freshness function that decreases
as Δ(𝑡ref,𝑡𝑑) increases, where𝑡ref is a reference time determined
by the scenario. This function serves as a recency-oriented feature
for the retriever, enabling it to identify the freshest version among
semantically similar documents. This captures the retriever’s ability
to prefer timely documents while avoiding staleness.

5 RE 2BENCH: RELEV ANCE & RECENCY
BENCHMARK
Existing IR benchmarks rarely test a model’s ability to handle both
temporal requirement and staleness resistance across the three
scenarios. Datasets exist for isolated aspects of TIR, such as time-
constrained QA for Relevance[6] and historical-version verification
for Recency[30]. However, they require additional processing before
they can serve as reliable testbeds for TIR. Moreover, standard IR
metrics such as R@K and MRR fail to capture the trade-off between
semantic matching and temporal constraints.
To address these gaps, we construct Re2Bench, a comprehensive
benchmark for TIR. Re 2Bench comprises three scenario-specific
subsets and introduces two novel evaluation metrics that explicitly
quantify a model’s capacity to resolve temporal constraints. This
benchmark provides the first standardized ground for evaluating
TIR models in all three scenarios.

To separately and jointly assess Align(𝑡𝑞,𝑡𝑐) and Fresh(𝑡𝑑,𝑡 ref),
Re2Bench is partitioned into three subsets, each corresponding to
a canonical TIR scenario defined in Section 3.

Table 4 presents the experiment results on the three subsets of

Scenario 1 (Re2-Rel): aligning to the query time without overfitting.

Scenario 2 (Re2-Rec): when recency dominates, restraint pays off
at the top rank. The recency-specialized tempRALM leads on MRR
and R@5 (0.768 and 0.857), which is expected given its hard-coded,
greedy recency prior; it consistently boosts newer items across the
candidate list, benefiting average reciprocal rank and top-𝐾 recall.

even in a recency-dominant setting, Re3 preserves enough semantic
discrimination to put the truly correct document first more often,
instead of broadly lifting many recent (but not always correct)
candidates. This “measured” behavior stems from the adaptive gate
learned by Re3: it increases the temporal influence when helpful, but
avoids overwhelming semantics. The same restraint that forgoes a
few wins on MRR in S2 becomes a decisive advantage once queries
require both semantic matching and recency in S3.
Scenario 3 (Re 2-Hyb): balancing both axes wins. Re3 achieves the
state-of-the-art R@1/R@5/MRR (0.742/0.971/0.836) and the lowest
TimeVar@5 (0.994), showing it simultaneously honors the query’s
temporal constraint and ranks the appropriate (often freshest) ver-
sion. TempRetriever attains the lowest MFG@5 (0.570), confirming
its strong freshness bias, but its accuracy lags, evidencing that a
rigid recency push is insufficient once semantic alignment is also
required. Overall, the soft-gated combination in Re3 learns when
to trust semantics and when to emphasize time, yielding superior
accuracy in the mixed regime.
The baselines follow a specialist strategy: models optimized for
a single axis perform strongly in the corresponding scenario—e.g.,
tempRALM and TempRetriever lead in Scenario 2 (Rec) on R@5
(0.857, 0.829) and MRR (0.768, 0.591), while TempRetriever attains
the lowest TimeVar@5 (0.881) in Scenario 1 (Rel). However, such
advantages are brittle and do not transfer when both challenges
co-occur. By contrast, Re3 functions as a generalist: it maintains
top-1 performance in Scenario 1 across all accuracy metrics (R@1

strong recency pressure in Scenario 2 (R@1 = 0.649, highest among

performance in Scenario 3, where it leads on all three accuracy met-

the lowest TimeVar@5 (0.994).

7 CONCLUSION
Temporal Information Retrieval (TIR) seeks to retrieve documents
that are both semantically relevant to a query and temporally ap-
propriate with respect to its constraints. This entails balancing
two intertwined challenges: Relevance, which requires aligning the
document’s content time with the query’s temporal requirement,
and Recency, which calls for selecting the most appropriate—often
freshest—version among multiple temporally staggered candidates.
While each challenge has been examined independently, existing
methods rarely address them in a unified and adaptive manner.
This work formalizes these dual challenges and presents Re 3,
a unified framework that decouples semantic and temporal mod-
eling while learning to combine them adaptively. Re 3 integrates
Fourier-based multi-scale time encoding, missing-aware processing,
and a learnable gating mechanism to achieve per-query balancing
between the two challenges. We further contribute Re 2Bench, a
benchmark comprising three subsets, each targeting Relevance, Re-
cency, or their hybrid combination. We also propose two diagnostic
metrics: TimeVar@K for Relevance and MFG@K for Recency.
Experiments on Re2Bench show that adaptive gating is critical
in hybrid scenarios where fixed-weight fusion underperforms, that
missing-aware encoding enhances robustness when timestamps
are incomplete, and that multi-frequencydelta-then-encode features
outperform naive scalar encoding and simple embedding subtrac-
tion, highlighting the importance of explicitly modeling temporal
gaps at multiple scales.
Nevertheless, several limitations remain. The method assumes
explicit or reliably extractable timestamps, which may not hold in
all settings. Evaluation is limited to three subsets, leaving gener-
alization to areas such as scientific literature or social media for
future work. Additionally, while re-ranking incurs minimal cost
for small candidate pools, latency may grow at very large 𝐾. Fu-
ture directions include relaxing timestamp assumptions, optimizing
for low-latency retrieval, expanding Re2Bench to broader domains
and tasks, and extending Re3 toward generative and multimodal
temporal retrieval.
ETHICAL CONSIDERATIONS
The proposed Re 3 framework is designed to enhance Temporal
Information Retrieval (TIR) by adaptively balancing recency and
relevance. While the objective is to improve retrieval accuracy and
efficiency, deploying such a system in real-world contexts may
entail societal and ethical risks. The following paragraphs outline
key concerns and possible mitigation strategies.
Information Bias and Amplification of Inequity. By leveraging a
learnable gating mechanism to balance recency and relevance, the
framework may inadvertently reflect or reinforce biases present
in the training data. For example, if recency correlates with a lim-
ited set of dominant sources, the model could disproportionately
prioritize these sources, thereby marginalizing alternative view-
points. Should these sources be systematically biased, the frame-
work could further amplify such biases, potentially affecting public
discourse and information equity. To mitigate this risk, bias audits
should be conducted prior to deployment, including evaluation on
diverse, representative, and multi-source datasets. Furthermore, in-
terpretability tools can help reveal how the model allocates weight
between recency and relevance, facilitating the identification and
correction of systematic bias.
Privacy Risks in Time-Sensitive Data. Although the Re 2Bench
dataset is derived from publicly available sources, applications in-
volving private datasets containing sensitive time-linked personal
information (e.g., location histories, health records) could pose sig-
nificant privacy risks. Unauthorized access or malicious exploitation
of such systems might result in privacy breaches. These risks can
be reduced by adhering to relevant data protection regulations (e.g.,
GDPR, CCPA) and incorporating safeguards such as anonymization,
de-identification, and differential privacy. A formal Privacy Impact
Assessment is strongly recommended prior to deployment.
Effects on Information Integrity. While prioritizing fresher infor-
mation can be advantageous, excessive emphasis on recency may
reduce exposure to more comprehensive or authoritative sources.
For instance, a recent but superficial news update could be ranked
above an older, more rigorous scientific work, potentially impair-
ing user understanding of a topic. This issue can be addressed by
allowing users to adjust the recency–relevance balance according
to their needs. In rapidly evolving contexts (e.g., news, weather),
higher recency weight may be desirable, whereas research-oriented
settings may warrant prioritizing relevance and depth.
By addressing these considerations, the Re3 framework can be
deployed in a manner that enhances retrieval performance while
safeguarding fairness, privacy, and informational integrity.
